# Schema Drift

*An algorithmic philosophy — the generative movement behind the artwork in this profile.*

## The movement

**Schema Drift** is a computational aesthetic concerned with a single transition: the moment
unstructured matter becomes structured meaning. Every piece begins in turbulence — a field of
particles with no obligation to anything but the noise that carries them — and ends in lattice,
where those same particles have been quantized, validated, and locked into a grid they did not
choose. Nothing is added between those two states. The order was always latent in the chaos; the
algorithm only raises the coherence until it becomes visible. Beauty here is not composed, it is
*extracted*.

## Chaos as a continuous field, not a phase

The system is governed by a coherence gradient — a scalar field that rises across the canvas.
Where coherence is near zero, particle heading is drawn entirely from layered Perlin noise, several
octaves deep, producing the long organic filaments of an unconstrained flow. As coherence climbs,
each heading is interpolated toward the nearest cardinal axis, and the step length snaps to the
lattice pitch. The transition is never a hard boundary: a particle at mid-coherence stutters between
curve and corner, drifting into structure the way a schema is inferred from examples rather than
declared. This gradient is the heart of the movement, and it demands a meticulously crafted
easing — a curve refined through countless iterations, because a transition placed a few percent
too early reads as a mistake and a few percent too late reads as two unrelated artworks sharing a
frame.

## Validation as luminance

Structure alone is not meaning. The lattice carries a mask — a set of cells that constitute a
constraint the field is being tested against. Particles that pass through a masked cell deposit
energy there; cells accumulate hits and brighten toward certainty, while unmasked cells that happen
to be visited remain dim residue, the honest evidence of paths that satisfied no constraint. Over
the run, a figure surfaces out of the noise field purely as a density map of successful traversals.
No glyph is ever drawn directly. It is *earned*, hit by hit, which is the entire conceptual
proposition: validated output is not authored, it is what survives the constraints.

## Color as a temperature of certainty

The palette is a thermometer for coherence, and it is deliberately narrow: paper, ink, and a single
ember. Warm dispersed pigment marks the entropic region — signal that has not yet been resolved.
Ink marks the validated region, because resolved signal should look like something that was set in
type rather than something that glows. Each trail is colored by the coherence at its own position,
so a single filament cools as it travels, and the composition's gradient becomes a read-out of the
algorithm's internal state. Opacity follows energy and certainty: young particles burn, settled ones
thin to residue so the ordered half stays quiet enough to read. Restraint is the discipline here —
three or four pigments, no more, each earning its place, none of them arbitrary RGB.

## Determinism as authorship

Every piece is a pure function of its seed. The same integer produces the same filaments, the same
brightening cells, the same residue, forever — on any machine, in any year. This is not a
convenience for reproducibility; it is the movement's claim about craft. A seed is a signature.
The artist's work is not in placing marks but in shaping the space of all possible marks so
completely that any seed drawn from it is worth keeping. That level of parametric control is the
product of deep computational expertise and painstaking optimization: force balance, noise octave
ratios, quantization easing, deposition falloff, and stroke weight are each tuned until the system
has no bad seeds, only different ones.

## The demand on the implementation

A Schema Drift piece must look like it took countless hours — because a master-level implementation
of it does. Density must be high enough to feel inevitable and low enough to stay legible. The
lattice must be felt before it is seen. The figure must resolve at a glance from across a room and
dissolve back into evidence when examined closely. Curves must be sampled finely enough that no
polyline betrays itself. Every constant in the algorithm should feel discovered rather than chosen —
the mark of someone at the absolute top of the field in computational aesthetics, who kept refining
long after the piece was merely good.
